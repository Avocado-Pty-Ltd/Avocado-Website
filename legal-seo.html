— 02. What changed in 2025–26

Search moved under your feet.
Here's what it means for an Australian firm.

AI Overviews & AI Mode

Answers now appear before the links.

AI Overviews reached Australia in late 2024 and Google rolled out AI Mode here in October 2025. Ahrefs' February 2026 study found the top organic result gets 58% fewer clicks when an Overview is present.

So: we write passages an AI can quote — a direct one-to-two sentence answer under every question heading — and we chase the clicks AI can't satisfy: "near me", "call now", "book a consult".

Map pack

Local relevance beats national muscle.

For "lawyer + suburb" searches, proximity and genuine local signals carry enormous weight. A boutique firm with a strong Google Business Profile and steady reviews can out-rank a national brand with ten times the backlinks.

So: we treat each office's Business Profile as a product — correct primary category, services, hours, photos, Q&A — and run a review program that never gates or incentivises.

Structured data

Your own star ratings won't show.

Since 2019 Google has ignored "self-serving" review markup on a business's own site — so the review-schema stars many agencies still sell won't appear for your firm.

So: we use LegalService and Person markup that ties lawyers, offices and practice areas together, and put review effort where it counts: Google itself.

Page experience

Most distress searches happen on a phone.

Interaction to Next Paint (INP) replaced First Input Delay as a Core Web Vital in March 2024. A slow, heavy practice page loses the reader before the phone number loads.

So: compressed images, lean templates, a sticky tap-to-call bar, and a sub-two-second mobile load target on every money page.

Digital PR

HARO isn't the shortcut it was.

Connectively (formerly HARO) shut down in December 2024; the relaunched HARO is now flooded with AI-written pitches. Journalist-quote link building needs local channels.

So: we pitch your lawyers as commentators through Australian channels such as SourceBottle and directly to local and regional press, where a link carries local weight.

Conduct rules

Marketing is regulated speech.

Rule 36 of the Australian Solicitors' Conduct Rules bans false or misleading advertising and "accredited specialist" claims without your state law society's accreditation. NSW, Queensland and the Northern Territory also restrict personal injury advertising, and the Australian Consumer Law applies everywhere.

So: no superlatives, no guaranteed outcomes, consent on every testimonial, and a practitioner sign-off step built into our publishing workflow.

— 03. Keyword research

From 400,000 raw searches
to one clear site map.

People in trouble don't type tidy keywords. They type "can my ex stop me seeing the kids" and "lawyer for drink driving Ringwood first offence". Our pipeline turns that noise into the exact pages your firm needs.

01Extract

Raw data extraction

Pull every query that could matter.

  • Competitor scrape — the top three local firms' pages and ranking terms
  • Your history — Search Console queries from your current site
  • Matching terms — broad seed exports, e.g. "lawyer" + "bail"
02Clean

Tokenise & clean

Break queries into single words and count them.

  • Frequency analysis — surfaces the words people pair with "lawyer": bail, licence, custody, TAC
  • Exclude rules — strip TV shows, other states and cities you don't serve
  • Persistent lists — exclusions reused across every export
03Cluster

SERP-overlap clustering

Let Google tell us which searches share an intent.

  • Bulk comparison — check the top-10 results for every keyword pair
  • Merge — high overlap means one page
  • Separate — low overlap means distinct pages
04Build

Site architecture

Prioritise by case value, then deploy.

  • Prioritise & filter — rank clusters by matter value and intent
  • Core money pages — one pillar per practice area and catchment
  • Sub-branches — focused pages linked back to the pillar

The 6-of-10 rule. Compare the top ten results for "bail lawyer Melbourne" and "bail application lawyer Melbourne". If six or more URLs appear in both, Google sees one intent — target both on a single page. If few overlap, build two. No cannibalisation, no thin duplicates.

— 04. Site architecture

One pillar per practice and place.
Everything else feeds it.

  1. 01

    The core money page

    One deep page for the commercial search — "family lawyer Dandenong" — that answers the real questions, shows the real people, and makes calling easy.

  2. 02

    Granular sub-pages

    Lower-volume, high-intent topics each get their own page. Individually small; together they prove to Google you cover the whole subject.

  3. 03

    Reciprocal linking

    Sub-pages link up to the pillar, the pillar links down, siblings link across. Any authority a sub-page earns flows into the page that wins the work.

  4. 04

    Compact pages for precise searches

    Short, transactional pages for ultra-specific queries — a single charge, a single court — where a 400-word answer and a phone number are exactly right.

— 06. Content protocol

AI drafts fast.
A lawyer signs off.

Deep pages used to take weeks. We build them from a verified knowledge base of your firm — case results, bios, reviews, local references — then nothing publishes until a practitioner has checked it.

Step 1 · AI

Knowledge base

Site map, practitioner bios, consented case outcomes, Google reviews and the legislation and court pages that matter in your state — one structured source of truth.

Step 2 · AI

Multi-prompt drafting

Outline built for passage ranking, then your firm's facts, then the full draft in clear, empathetic language. Fast — and the riskiest stage, so it never publishes alone.

Step 3 · Human

Practitioner compliance edit

Every statute, time limit and court reference verified. No "best", no guarantees, no specialist claims without law society accreditation. Signed off by your lawyer.

Step 4 · Publish

Publish, mark up, request indexing

LegalService and Person schema, internal links to the pillar, the URL resubmitted in Search Console, and the page added to the refresh calendar.

AI provides the velocity. Your lawyers provide the safety.
In legal search you can't have one without the other.

— Compliance by design

Built around the rules
Australian lawyers work under.

  • ASCR rule 36. Nothing false, misleading, deceptive or offensive — so no "best lawyer in Sydney" and no implied guarantees.
  • Specialist claims. "Accredited specialist" appears only where a lawyer holds accreditation from their state law society or institute.
  • Personal injury. In NSW, Queensland and the Northern Territory, personal injury pages follow the state limits on advertising those services.
  • Australian Consumer Law. Testimonials are genuine, current and consented; "no win, no fee" terms are explained in full.
  • Client confidentiality. Case results are de-identified unless the client has given written consent.
  • Your sign-off. Your firm remains responsible for its advertising, so a practitioner approves every page before it goes live.

This is how we work, not legal advice. Your firm should confirm its own obligations with its state's legal services regulator or law society.

— 12. Questions

What law firms ask us first.

How long until we see results?

Business Profile and technical fixes often move map-pack visibility within the first two to three months. Competitive practice pages typically take six to twelve months to reach and hold page one, depending on your catchment and competitors. We report on calls and enquiries from month one so you can see the trend, not just the rankings.

Do you guarantee a #1 ranking?

No — and you should be wary of anyone who does. Google controls the rankings. We commit to the work, the process and transparent reporting on the outcomes that matter: qualified calls, consultations and signed matters.

Is AI-written content safe for a law firm?

Only with a human in the loop. We use AI to draft from a verified knowledge base of your firm, then every statute, time limit and claim is checked and a practitioner signs off. Unchecked AI content risks wrong law, misleading advertising and quality penalties from Google.

We're a regional firm. Is this worth it?

Usually, yes — at the right tier. Smaller catchments rarely justify an aggressive content campaign, but they're often the easiest map packs to win. Local Foundations is built for exactly that: owning your town's "lawyer near me" searches.

Can we put "Family Lawyers" in our Google Business Profile name?

Only if it's part of your real-world business name. Adding keywords that aren't in your name breaks Google's guidelines and can get a Profile suspended. If a descriptive name genuinely suits your practice, that's a business-name decision to make properly first — then Google follows.

Do we have to use EzyBiz LEX or Call Concierge?

No. Growth and Leadership need reliable 24/7 intake, but it can be your own team or another provider. Our products are simply the fastest way to get there, and they connect to Smokeball and Clio.

Who owns the website and content?

You do. The site, the pages, the Business Profile, the tracking numbers and the reporting accounts are set up in your firm's name. If you leave, everything stays with you.

What does the free search audit include?

A 30-minute call, then a written summary: how you show up today for your key practice-and-suburb searches, how your Business Profile compares with your top three local competitors, your intake readiness, and which tier (if any) we'd recommend.
